## 0.23.0 (2026-10-15)

- Study S4 (new, pre-registered): **can retuning make two feedback models agree on every galaxy observable at once?** IllustrisTNG against SIMBA in CAMELS: 169 binned galaxy-population outputs in 35 blocks (11 families at z = 0, 0.95, 2.15, 3, 4; 186 of 530 outputs eligible), a distance in units of the noise left when everything else is fixed (1 = the standard deviation of the difference between one run of each code), minimised over the four parameters of each code. The protocol (`docs/PROTOCOL-joint-reachability.md`) fixes five questions and their rules before any outcome existed; one amendment (A1), after an independent review and before the run, replaced the distance by one with the emulators' own error at the minimiser removed in quadrature, compared the joint-to-separate ratio with the same ratio for two halves of one code, and added a decomposition by block and a calibration on synthetic worlds with known truth (`scripts/power_joint_reachability.py`, `docs/POWER-joint-reachability.md`: 0 of 17 runs with a true distance below 0.95 were called structural, 8 of 8 above 1.1; the uncorrected distance is biased up by 0.14 to 0.16 at zero true distance).
- Result (`docs/ANALYSIS-joint-reachability.md`, one run, no deviations): **J1 structural**, 4.8 noise units apart at the best joint setting (half-sample 4.6 to 4.9; 6.3 at the fiducial parameters; same-code controls zero after correction). **J2** fitted alone, black hole occupation (9.5) and gas metallicity at fixed stellar mass (1.7) stay structural; six families are removable (stellar mass function, quenched fraction, black hole mass, stellar half-mass radius, gas fraction, stellar mass and SFR density); three are unresolved (star-forming main sequence, stellar metallicity, stellar-to-halo mass ratio), of which two and the half-mass radius turn structural when the search is kept to 75 % of the range. **J3** the squared joint distance is 2.8 times that of separately tuned families against 2.0 (95th percentile 2.7) for two halves of one code, so the rule does not call a conflict; the mechanism is classed irreducible because a family is structural by itself. **J4** leaving out any one family leaves 3.7 to 5.2; without the two black hole families 3.8. **J5** 2.8, 4.2 and 6.9 at z = 0, 0.95 and 2.15 (fewer and only more massive bins at higher z). All five validations hold (including the halo mass function as a negative control). The two structural families are seeding (a discrete model choice) and enrichment, not tuning; the conventional calibration targets can be matched alone, so they do not tell the two codes apart.
- Data (new kind): `data/ensembles/camels-galaxies.json`, binned galaxy-population summaries of 2054 CAMELS simulations at five snapshots (stellar mass function, quenched fraction, specific SFR, black hole mass and occupation, gas and stellar metallicity, half-mass radius, gas fraction, stellar-to-halo mass ratio, halo mass function, cosmic densities), read from the public Subfind catalogues by HTTP range requests (about 1 MB instead of 15 to 50 MB per file) with the SHA-256 of every array read (`scripts/ingest_camels_galaxies.py`, tested on a synthetic catalogue by `scripts/test_camels_galaxies.py`).
- Site (redesigned): a journal-and-instrument visual system (serif text, monospaced labels, no cards, no shadows, ink and one accent), a new information architecture (Explorer, **Retune**, Studies, Data, Methods) and every study as a paper-format page with abstract, status, registration, figures and reproduction commands. The app is re-themed to match. The marketing-style front page and the findings summary are replaced (the old findings address redirects); the numbers on the study pages are read from the analysis JSON.
- Retune (new instrument): `site/retune.html` evaluates the S4 emulators in the browser (degree 2 or 3 polynomials at the fiducial cosmology, `data/retune/retune.json`, 272 kB): four dials per code, 11 observable families as panels with seed-scatter and reachable bands and published curves of the dataset as context, a joint distance that reproduces the study's numbers (6.35 at the fiducial parameters, 4.85 at its best setting; asserted by a browser test), matching one code to the other or both free, and the dial state in the address.
- Usability: the front page now leads with the three things one does with the dataset (look up a relation, check your own run, take the data) instead of the studies, which move below the data; the Explorer's *Your run* panel opens from a link (`#...&pn=mine`), has a *Try an example* button (a published curve shifted by +0.15, labelled as such) so the comparison can be seen without data of one's own, and states the range and median of the offsets against the curves whose definitions agree.
- S4 in physical units (descriptive, not part of the registered result): a table of the rms difference between the codes in dex or in fraction for every family at the fiducial parameters, at its own best setting and at the best joint setting, and the size of one noise unit per family (0.04 to 0.18 dex, 0.05 to 0.08 for the families measured as fractions). At the best joint setting the codes differ by 0.19 dex in the stellar mass function (0.08 tuned alone), 0.49 dex in black hole mass (0.14) and 0.60 in the black hole occupation fraction (0.50).
- Retune in plain language: dials named for what they do (supernova wind strength, black hole feedback strength, supernova wind speed, black hole jet speed) with the technical name beneath; a live sentence ("the two universes agree on N of 11 things; furthest apart on ..."); after each match button, a short story of what moved, what came into line, what was knocked out of agreement and what stayed apart; the default match is one property at a time, which makes the trade-offs visible (tuning for one property usually breaks others), and the verdict column reads "no dial setting closes it / can be closed / borderline". ## 0.22.0 (2026-10-14)

- Analysis (new, pre-registered): **parametric versus structural uncertainty in the cosmic star formation history of simulated galaxy formation.** What can a simulation still do by retuning its own parameters, and what does its form force whatever the tuning? The protocol (`docs/PROTOCOL-model-form.md`, committed before any outcome was computed) asks four questions: E1 whether published simulations disagree more at z = 5 to 7.5 than at 0.5 to 3 (a "funnel"); E2 how big the authors' own resolution, parameter and cosmology variants are beside the disagreement between codes; E3 whether IllustrisTNG can reproduce SIMBA by retuning four feedback parameters in the CAMELS experiment (about 1000 simulations per code at identical resolution), compared with the same code against itself; E4 whether the two codes, calibrated to the same observed star formation history at z <= 3, still disagree at z = 4 to 8. One amendment (A1), written before any outcome after an independent review of the protocol and code, replaced the E3 verdict by matched-size emulators with a seed-scatter margin, put a common systematic and an effective-sample-size floor into E4, added a volume check to V1, corrected the ridge, V4 and V5 and the disclosure of what had been looked at, and replaced the power table by the output of a committed script (`scripts/power_model_form.py`, `docs/POWER-model-form.md`).
- Result (`docs/ANALYSIS-model-form.md`, generated by `scripts/analyze_model_form.py`): **E1 no funnel detected** (seven balanced families; spread 0.17 dex at z = 0.5 to 3 and 0.37 dex at z = 5 to 7.5, a factor 2.2; 90 % bootstrap interval of the log ratio -0.27 to 0.61; Pitman-Morgan p = 0.066; the rule needs both; and leaving FLAMINGO out reverses the sign: its flagship-resolution run is the lowest curve above z = 5 and the same code at eight times the mass resolution lies roughly a dex higher, a reading added after the result). **E3 removable by the rule fixed in advance**: at their fiducial parameters the two codes differ by 0.22 dex rms in CAMELS; retuning brings them to 0.08 dex (0.01 at z < 3.1, 0.06 above), far above the emulation noise (at most 0.012) but below the 0.13 dex by which two seeds of these codes differ; the best-matching pair sits at the edge of the CAMELS range in 3 of its 8 coordinates (and within 0.1 of it in 2 more), and with the ranges cut to a quarter the codes stay 0.13 dex apart. **E4 parametric**: calibrated to Madau and Dickinson (2014) at z <= 3 the 90 % intervals overlap at every z = 4.1 to 7.7 although the medians drift apart by up to 0.42 dex. E2: within a code the authors' resolution and box changes move the SFRD by 0.23 dex (z < 3) and 0.37 dex (z > 3) rms, as much as the codes differ from each other. **Validations: V4 for SIMBA fails marginally** (cross-validation error 1.51 times the seed noise at z = 1.7 against a limit of 1.5), so E3 and E4 are read with that qualification; V1 (also with the new volume check), V2, V3, V5 hold. Sensitivities S1 to S9 leave the E1 and E4 verdicts unchanged and the E3 distance between 0.08 and 0.11 dex (below the 0.13 dex margin). The power study says what this can see: with seven families a funnel of a factor three is found about two times in three; a structural verdict needs a step between the codes of about 0.6 dex (weak parameter responses) to 1 dex that the four parameters cannot absorb.
- Data (vector figures; 34 records): **IllustrisTNG model variants** (Pillepich+18; 18 records: the fiducial model, stronger and faster winds, no wind velocity floor, no metal-line cooling, the Illustris model at two resolutions and others, with cross-figure duplicate checks) and **FLAMINGO** (Schaye+23; 16 records: the flagship and the m8, m10 and 2.8 Gpc runs, calibration, cosmology and jet variants).
- Data (new kind): `data/ensembles/camels-sfrd.json`, the cosmic star formation rate density of 2054 CAMELS simulations (IllustrisTNG and SIMBA, 1000 Latin-hypercube and 27 cosmic-variance simulations each) reduced from the codes' own star formation logs in redshift bins of 0.2, with the SHA-256 of every file read (`scripts/ingest_camels_sfrd.py`, `scripts/test_camels_ingest.py`), and `data/model-variants.json`, which fixes for 11 model families the fiducial curve and 37 variants with their class (numerical, parametric, calibration, cosmology, component, implementation, ablation) and the cited change. Closure check of the units and the volume: `scripts/validate_camels_closure.py`.
- Correction to the 0.21.0 harmonization analysis: its IMF step applied the website's IMF offsets as if they were a definitional difference between simulations. They are a convention for comparing a simulated star formation or stellar mass with an observation; a simulation's mass function is the mass of stars its model formed, a property of the model. The offsets are small against the other terms and the conclusion (not detected) does not depend on them, but the framing is withdrawn; the new analysis applies no IMF conversion to simulated quantities (only to observations and in sensitivity S4).
- Site: the Findings page gains the parametric-versus-structural section (the funnel, the variant tiers, the CAMELS decomposition and the calibration chart), all generated from `data/analysis/model-form.json`.
- Tooling: `scripts/modelform_stats.py` (Pitman-Morgan test, Gaussian-process draws, weighted ridge emulator; numpy only), `scripts/test_model_form.py` (synthetic tests incl. an end-to-end twin world), `scripts/design_check_model_form.py` and its output (the check of the CAMELS file made before the protocol, kept for the record). The analysis takes about 20 minutes, so `verify-all.sh` runs it only with `FULL=1`; the test always checks that its saved output is not stale.

## 0.21.0 (2026-10-14)

- Analysis (new, pre-specified): **how much of the disagreement between simulated z about 0 stellar mass functions do documented definitions explain?** The protocol (`docs/PROTOCOL-harmonization.md`) was committed before any statistic existed; two amendments (A1: a mass-scatter operator with a validation gate; A2: a galaxy-level aperture remap with a Jacobian, prompted by a synthetic test that showed fixed-abundance shifts are not transferable between curves) are dated and say what had been seen when each was made, including that an E3 had been computed under the superseded design. The analysis (`scripts/analyze_harmonization.py`, output `data/analysis/harmonization.json` and the generated `docs/ANALYSIS-harmonization.md`) uses the stellar mass at fixed abundance as the coordinate, one curve per suite chosen by the website's own fiducial rule (ported to Python and tested against the JavaScript), the standard IMF offsets, a Richardson-Lucy removal of FLAMINGO's stated 0.3 dex mass scatter (validated against COLIBRE's released tables with and without Eddington scatter: at most 0.035 dex), and aperture remaps measured inside single EAGLE, COLIBRE and Illustris runs.
- Result: **not detected.** With 8 suites, R (the share of the between-suite variance of m(phi) that disappears) is 0.15 (95 % cluster-bootstrap interval -1.04 to 0.51; label-permutation p = 0.11; the sign differs in sensitivity S2), so the pre-specified rule is not met. By operator alone: IMF 0.30, mass scatter -0.32 (removing FLAMINGO's stated scatter moves it away from the others), aperture -0.00; only 2 of 8 suites need an aperture correction and it is about 0.01 dex. The upper bound if the three suites with an undocumented aperture took the best library aperture is R = 0.16, not above the null of meaningless maps (95th percentile 0.28). The 30 pkpc to total map is suite dependent (Illustris differs from EAGLE and COLIBRE). The injection-recovery power analysis shows that no tested size of aperture effect, up to twice the measured maps, is detected in 80 % of draws (false-positive rate 0 %): the suites could not have seen an aperture effect of this size, so the result bounds what the data can show and says nothing for or against such an effect. SIMBA, whose per-dex normalisation is not stated, enters only in the sensitivity analysis S7.
- Data (vector figures; 9 records): the within-run aperture comparisons the analysis needs. **EAGLE** Ref-L100N1504 at z = 0.1 (Schaye+15): the stellar mass function with stellar mass in 10, 30 and 100 pkpc spherical apertures, a 2D Petrosian aperture and no aperture (5 records; the figure's legend says 10, 30 and 100 pkpc while its caption says 30, 50 and 100, and the legend, which is what is drawn, is used and flagged). **COLIBRE** L200m6 at z = 0 (Chaikin+26): 30, 50, 100 pkpc and all bound stars (4 records). Cross-checks against independent sources: the COLIBRE curves reproduce the released table to 0.000 dex and the EAGLE 30 pkpc curve agrees with the Furlong+15 record to 0.011 dex at the shared points. The records are excluded from the choice of one curve per suite.
- Site: the Findings page gains the harmonization result (chart of the spread before and after, R with its interval and permutation p, the operators alone and the power statement), all generated from `data/analysis/harmonization.json`.
- Tooling: `vector_figure.reference_root()` lets a digitizer cross-check against the committed records when run from a scratch copy (the regression harness sets it); `scripts/fiducial-oracle.mjs` prints what the website's `primaryUnits` picks; `scripts/analyze_harmonization.py` and `scripts/test_harmonization.py` join the verification chain (the test checks the machinery on synthetic Schechter functions: recovery of known maps and Jacobians, false-positive rate under no effect, detection of injected effects, the E3 rule, and that the saved outputs are not stale).
- Limits stated in the analysis: three codes in the aperture library, 30 to 50 pkpc rests on COLIBRE alone, the "total" mass is a family, curves are digitized (0.01 to 0.02 dex), cubic remaps cannot follow a sharp bend, suites are treated as exchangeable, and nothing is learned about other epochs or relations.

## 0.16.0 (2026-10-13)

- New analysis: definition budget (`scripts/analyze_definition_budget.py`, `data/analysis/definition-budget.json`, `docs/ANALYSIS-definition-budget.md`). Cross-suite record pairs are classified from documented definitions alone (matched, differing, unknown, not comparable) and their offsets compared, with a label permutation test and a bootstrap over suites; no curve is corrected. On the present records 4% of 2255 pairs are fully matched and 33% cannot be classified because a definition is undocumented, and the two nominally significant epochs do not survive Bonferroni correction. Tested by `scripts/test_definition_budget.py`.
