**sim-highline: check whether two simulated galaxy curves can be compared, and see what the feedback dials of TNG and SIMBA can and cannot change**

I have made a free, open tool and would like criticism from people who run or use these simulations. https://shubhanbhatia42.github.io/sim-highline/

- **1,943 curves** of galaxy scaling relations from 20 simulations and 114 observational and empirical sources, each with the definitions that decide comparability (mass aperture, IMF, population, SFR timescale) and its source. CC BY 4.0, code MIT, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23217259.
- **Check your own run:** paste a curve, get offsets against the published curves that are fair to compare with, as a table and CSV. Runs in your browser.
- **The dials page:** emulators of 2,054 CAMELS runs (IllustrisTNG and SIMBA). One result there: after the best possible retuning of each code's four feedback dials, they stay 4.8 noise units apart over all properties at once (a noise unit is the scatter between one run of each code), driven by black hole occupation and gas metallicity; 6 of 11 properties can be matched. The decision rule was committed before the result (see the registration trail on the studies page) and the checks made afterwards are listed as exploratory.

Caveats up front: unreviewed; 54% of simulation curves are digitized from published figures by me and never used for ranking; CAMELS resolution; two codes (a third, Astrid, is in an exploratory check). I did not find prior work on the specific question, but I have only read abstracts, and CAMELS already reports that the two codes differ.

**What I am asking:** (1) where is it wrong, (2) where does it not do what you need, (3) if you are an author of a curve in it, is the reading right? Issues welcome: https://github.com/ShubhanBhatia42/sim-highline/issues/new?template=feedback.yml
