## Short (a post, a Slack message)

I have built sim-highline, a free tool for comparing simulated galaxy scaling relations with each other and with observations. 1,883 curves from 20 simulations and 109 observational and empirical sources, each with the definitions (mass aperture, IMF, population) that decide whether two curves can fairly be compared. You can paste your own simulation's curve and see which published curves it is comparable with, and turn the four feedback dials of IllustrisTNG and SIMBA (from 2,054 CAMELS runs) to see what moves. Nothing leaves your browser. It is an unreviewed beta: 54% of the simulation curves were read from published figures by one person, and I would like to hear where it is wrong. https://shubhanbhatia42.github.io/sim-highline/

Subject: A tool for checking whether two simulated galaxy curves can be compared

Hello,

I maintain sim-highline (https://shubhanbhatia42.github.io/sim-highline/), an open dataset and browser tool for galaxy scaling relations from cosmological simulations. It does three things:

1. **Look up a relation** (stellar mass function, size, metallicity, black hole mass and others) at any epoch: every published simulation and observation on one plot, each labelled with where it came from and whether its definitions allow a fair comparison with the rest.
2. **Check your own run:** paste a curve, say what it measures, and get offsets against the published curves that are comparable with it, as a table and a CSV.
3. **Turn the physics dials:** emulators of 2,054 CAMELS simulations of IllustrisTNG and SIMBA, evaluated in the browser. It also answers a question I could not find answered: after the best possible retuning of each code's four feedback dials, the two codes are still 4.8 noise units apart over all properties at once, with 2 properties that stay apart at every setting tested (black hole occupation by a wide margin, gas metallicity by a small one) and 6 that can be matched. The rule for that was written before the result and is on the site, together with the checks I made afterwards.

What it is not: peer reviewed, a leaderboard, or complete. 54% of the simulation curves are read from published figures by one maintainer and are never used to rank anything; where an author or collaboration publishes a table, I would rather use that and will gladly swap it in. Nobody outside the project has used it yet that I know of, which is why I am writing: **where does it fail you?** A reply of two sentences, or an issue at https://github.com/ShubhanBhatia42/sim-highline/issues/new?template=feedback.yml, is the most useful thing you could send.

Shubhan Bhatia
