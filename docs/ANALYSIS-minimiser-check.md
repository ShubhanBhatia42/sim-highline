# Are the emulators trustworthy at the registered minimum? (exploratory)

Generated by `scripts/explore_minimiser_check.py` after the registered run; it changes no verdict. The registered search puts its minimum at the edge of the dial box (4 of the 8 dial coordinates on a bound), where the emulators are least constrained. For each code, the 15 simulations whose dial settings are nearest the minimising setting were **held out**, the emulator was refitted without them, and their predicted outputs were compared with what the simulations actually gave, in the analysis' noise units over the 169 outputs of the all-at-once set. The same was done for the 15 simulations nearest the fiducial setting and for 15 random simulations. A single simulation's own seed scatter is part of every number (about 0.7 noise units is what perfect emulation would give), so the comparison between rows is what matters.

## Reading

If the corner rows are close to the others, held-out simulations there are predicted about as well as anywhere, and the emulators' own error does not by itself explain the distance. If they are clearly worse, the distance at the edge is less certain than the half-sample ranges say. Two limits: the nearest simulations are not *at* the minimum (the distance column says how far they are; a six-parameter Latin hypercube of 1,000 points is sparse in a corner), and the simulations nearest the minimum differ in cosmology too, which the emulator is asked to handle as well. This is a check on prediction error near the corner, not a simulation at the minimum.
