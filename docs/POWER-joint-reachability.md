# Calibration of the joint-reachability estimators on synthetic worlds

Generated by `scripts/power_joint_reachability.py` (seed 20261017); nothing here reads a real catalogue. Protocol: [`PROTOCOL-joint-reachability.md`](PROTOCOL-joint-reachability.md), amendment A1.

Worlds have the block layout of the real eligible outputs (35 joint blocks, 169 outputs, plus the halo mass function control; z = 0, 0.95, 2.15, 3, 4), heteroscedastic noise (seed scatter 0.05 to 0.2), 4 % missing values, 1000 + 27 simulations per code with independent designs, and responses of 3 to 30 noise units. The *true* distance is computed from the noise-free responses with the weights and noise scale of the analysis (so it is the quantity the verdict should recover; 1 is the margin). Verdicts are those of the analysis (emulator-corrected) and, for comparison, the same rule on the uncorrected distributions (raw). *corner*: the codes agree exactly, but only with the first code at a corner of its parameter box (radius r) and the second at its fiducial point; *offset*: every output of the second code is off by a fixed number of noise units; *shift*: one family (black hole occupation) is off; *conflict*: every family reachable alone by its own parameter pair.

## Reading

Among the 17 runs whose true joint distance is below 0.95 (the codes agree, up to 0.60 noise units), 0 receive a structural joint verdict with the emulator-corrected rule of the analysis and 0 with the uncorrected one; among the 8 runs above 1.1 (up to 6.65), 8 and 8 do. The rule therefore separates worlds on the two sides of the margin in this design, including a single shifted family whose joint distance is only 1.16. The uncorrected distances are biased upwards by 0.14 to 0.16 where the true distance is zero (the emulators' own error at the minimiser), which the verdict tolerates because the same-code controls carry the same bias; the corrected distances remove it and the 75 % range does not change a verdict in any world. A world at the margin (true distance 0.98) is called removable and one at 1.48 structural: nothing in the design says what happens between, and a true distance close to 1 is a coin toss by construction. *Families structural alone* are the J2 verdicts of the families fitted one at a time; in the offset worlds every output is off by the same number of noise units, but the dials absorb part of it in some families, so at 1.5 each run has two or three families that are unresolved or removable alone and are not listed. J3 distinguishes *irreducible* (a family is structural alone) from *conflict* (every family is removable alone and the joint distance is structural, above the same ratio for two halves of one code) and is empty if the joint verdict is not structural. V2 is the halo mass function negative control of the protocol: it is not shifted in the shift worlds (removable) and is offset like everything else in the offset worlds (structural at 1.5).
