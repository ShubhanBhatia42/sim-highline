# A review of the whole project, 2026-11-20, and what was done about it

A review of the whole project made at the maintainer's request, with the public numbers, the live site and the data as they were that day. The numbers will drift; this note is not updated.

## What the review found

- **Nobody outside the project had used it.** 0 stars, 0 forks, 0 watchers, no issue from a person (the open ones are the weekly paper list); the repository page had 2 views from 1 visitor in two weeks; no outside audit outcome, no pilot session, no author reply. The site has no analytics, so site visits are unknown.
- **The first minute did not invite.** The front page opened with a claim about definitions and no figure; the Explorer opened under a large instruction box with a legend that showed two curve names; the dials page buried its instrument under an essay; on a phone the title was a screen down.
- **A wrong citation was on every page.** The version chip and the "Cite as" line paired v0.24.0 with a DOI that leads to the Zenodo record of v0.15.0, the only version deposited.
- **Little could be compared.** 13 of the 44 relations had no simulation curve at all (observations only); 21 had fewer than 6; only 13 relations had five or more simulation sources and an observation. In the Explorer's simulations-against-observations map, across six reference redshifts, 0 of 211 cells were cleanly comparable, 42 comparable with caveats and 169 not comparable. 45 % of all records, and 55 % of simulation curves, were read from figures, which are never used for ranking.
- **The studies are largely null results**, said so on the front page, and are not what brings a visitor in.

## What was done (see the changelog for the detail)

1. **The first minute.** The front page opens with a figure from the data, one plain sentence with its numbers and one primary button; phones get a narrower copy of the figure. The DOI wording is derived from a new field (`archivedVersion` in `data/release.json`) on every page, file and draft. The Explorer opens with a one-line note and a legend that shows about 13 simulations; the reading key is closed; relations with no simulation curve are listed apart; the view tabs fit on a phone. Each of these has a test that fails when it is undone.
2. **Three answers.** `answers.html` and three pages, each a question answered from the data with the Explorer's own rules, generated and tested against the raw records and against the Explorer. Two of the three, the UV luminosity function and the mass–metallicity relation at higher redshift, show how little can be compared: the pages say so and give the reasons.
3. **Depth where comparison is possible.** 89 same-definition curves from the CAMELS public catalogues for three codes and six relations (the black hole relation goes from 2 to 5 rankable simulation sources). Replacing figure reads by author tables is mostly closed: of the papers behind the figure-read curves, the earlier search found tables released for few of them (196 records are "announced, not released", 92 "on request", 98 "none found", 272 need a recomputation from catalogues most of which need an account); the requests are drafted (`docs/author-requests.md`) and wait for a person to send them.

## What is still open, and whose it is

- **People (the maintainer).** Three pilot sessions with people who did not watch it being built; one expert per simulation to check two records; the author requests; naming a backup maintainer; publishing the release and setting `archivedVersion` (`docs/SEND-THIS-WEEK.md`). Nothing here substitutes for them, and the first minute can only be judged by someone seeing it for the first time.
- **The stellar mass function from the CAMELS catalogues** is not in the dataset. The pre-registered harmonization study takes one curve per simulation source from every stellar mass function record, so a new source with a z ≈ 0 mass function would change a frozen result; it needs a decision about the study, not a quiet workaround.
- **Large-box homogeneous measurements** (the production runs, measured the same way) need catalogues behind accounts or on request, or hundreds of gigabytes; none was started.
- **The UV luminosity function cannot be compared under the current rules** because the UV bands differ (1500 Å, 1600 Å, 150 nm). Whether a band difference of that size should block a comparison is a decision about the rules, which also feed a descriptive analysis; it was not changed.
- **Only 13 relations support a real comparison.** The others are thin and the Explorer says so; making them deep is the long job.
