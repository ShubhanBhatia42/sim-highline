## Re-digitizing a random sample by a different method

The original extraction reads the vector paths of the figure. To test it without those paths, a second reader rendered each figure to a 400 dpi raster, recalibrated the axes from the tick marks in the image, and traced the curve by colour (`data/analysis/redigitization-check.json`). Curves were drawn at random until 20 could be checked; 96 were tried, and 76 could not be checked (overlapping curves of one colour, raster or outlined axes, or a stored quantity that is a transform of the plotted one). Of the 20 checked, from 8 papers, 10 are within tolerance and 10 are not. On the 12 curves with logarithmic y axes the median of the per-curve median differences is 0.0006 dex and the 90th percentile of the per-curve maxima is 0.15 dex; 4 exceed the extraction's stated uncertainty. Each failure was inspected: none could be attributed to a stored curve (a marker, error bar or other stroke of the same colour lay on the curve, or the panel holds many near-identical curves), one is inconclusive at about 0.01 dex. This checks the extraction of the curves it could check (21% of those tried); it is not a statement about the rest, it cannot see a wrong panel or run, or a wrong tick label shared by both methods, and one reader made it.


## Did the authors release the numbers?

For the 49 papers behind the 792 figure-read simulation curves, `data/analysis/released-tables.json` records whether the numbers were published or released. Result: the numbers of 3 paper(s) are fully available as a table, 5 partly; for 24 papers (272 curves) only the simulation's catalogues are public, so the plotted numbers would have to be recomputed; 5 papers say the data are available on request or will be released (288 curves, among them the largest block); for 12 nothing was found. Of the 10 curves for which a table that matches the plotted numbers exactly was found, 10 have been replaced by the table's numbers (their tier is now published-table or official-table). 1 more figure-read curve is a duplicate of numbers already in the dataset as an official table (`chaikin26.colibre.l200m6.gsmf-ap-50pkpc.z0`); it is kept because the figure shows an aperture series.

## Source checksums

1792 of 1960 records carry the SHA-256 of the file they were made from. The digitizer regression re-fetches every source and compares; the records without a checksum are transcribed fits and tables.

## Spelling of definitions

Comparison rules match definition values as strings, so two spellings of one thing count as different. Spellings that differ only in case, punctuation or filler words:

- **population**: 78 distinct values over 1870 records; 3 groups of near-duplicates, for example `all` (792) / `all galaxies` (119); `star-forming` (169) / `all galaxies, star-forming` (8); `passive` (40) / `all galaxies, passive` (8)
- **massDefinition**: 51 distinct values over 1594 records; 1 groups of near-duplicates, for example `aperture-0.1Rvir` (36) / `aperture-0.1rvir` (36)
- **imf**: 4 distinct values over 1832 records; no near-duplicates
- **densityFrame**: 2 distinct values over 660 records; no near-duplicates
- **sizeDefinition**: 17 distinct values over 214 records; no near-duplicates
- **Consequence**: population is a hard key for sfms (0 of 446 cross-simulation pairs near the same epoch differ only in spelling), ssfr (0 of 471 cross-simulation pairs near the same epoch differ only in spelling), size (53 of 2149 cross-simulation pairs near the same epoch differ only in spelling). Such pairs are judged not comparable only because of how the population was written.

Changing the vocabulary changes the inputs of the definitions and mass-function studies, whose rules were fixed in advance, so it is recorded here and not changed silently.
