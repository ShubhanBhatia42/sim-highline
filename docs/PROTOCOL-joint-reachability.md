# Protocol: can retuning make two feedback models agree on every galaxy observable at once?

Status: **pre-specified**. Written on 2026-10-14 after the data were ingested and their availability checked (section 8 lists exactly what had been looked at) and **before** any response of an observable to a parameter, any reachability distance, any verdict or any selection of observables was computed. The analysis code (`scripts/analyze_joint_reachability.py`) implements this text; every departure is listed under "Deviations" in `docs/ANALYSIS-joint-reachability.md`, which the code generates and which reports whatever comes out, including a null result. **Amended once, by A1 (section 10), after an independent review of this text and the code (synthetic experiments and checks of single-simulation inputs only) and before any outcome was computed; where A1 and sections 1 to 9 differ, A1 governs.**

## 1. The question

The previous study (`PROTOCOL-model-form.md`) found that the cosmic star formation history alone cannot separate IllustrisTNG from SIMBA in the CAMELS experiment: retuning four feedback parameters brings the two to within the scatter between seeds. A single observable is a poor test, because one observable constrains few combinations of four parameters. Real calibrations use many observables at once, and models that agree on one can disagree on another.

Define, for a code c, the map y_c(a) from its four astrophysical parameters a (in [-1, 1]^4) to a vector of galaxy-population summaries y (section 3). The set of all y_c(a) is the code's *reachable set*. The **structural distance** between two codes is the distance between their reachable sets, in units of the noise that remains when everything is fixed:

    D(S) = min over (a_TNG, a_SIMBA) of sqrt( mean over the outputs k in S of w_k ((y_TNG,k(a_TNG) - y_SIMBA,k(a_SIMBA)) / s_k)^2 ).

Both codes are read from catalogues made by the same Subfind code with the same field list (checked on one simulation: the same Subfind commit, the same field names, the same radii definitions) in the same box at the same resolution, so no aperture, IMF or resolution difference enters. What is left is the difference between the codes: their subgrid models, but also their hydrodynamic solvers, star formation algorithms, yields and wind treatment (A1 lists the checked differences).

Questions:

* **J1 (joint).** Is the distance for *all* observables together, with one parameter pair per code, above the noise margin (structural), below it (removable) or neither?
* **J2 (each alone).** For which observables is the distance structural even when that observable alone is fitted? These cannot be absorbed by any retuning.
* **J3 (the cost of agreeing).** How much larger is the squared joint distance than the weighted mean of the squared distances of the observables fitted one at a time? That excess is the retuning conflict: no single parameter pair serves all observables.
* **J4 (what separates them).** Which observables carry the separation: the order in which adding observables raises the joint distance, the smallest set found by that order whose distance is structural, and the distance without each family.
* **J5 (redshift).** Does the structural distance grow with redshift (the "funnel" of the previous study, now for the joint distance)?

## 2. Data

`data/ensembles/camels-galaxies.json`, written by `scripts/ingest_camels_galaxies.py` from the public CAMELS Subfind catalogues (`groups_NNN.hdf5`) by HTTP range reads of the needed arrays (the SHA-256 of the arrays' bytes is stored for every simulation and snapshot): IllustrisTNG and SIMBA, 1000 Latin-hypercube simulations each (LH; four astrophysical parameters and two cosmological; each with its own seed), and 27 cosmic-variance simulations each (CV; fiducial parameters, 27 seeds), at the snapshots z = 0, 0.95, 2.15, 3.0 and 4.0. The boxes are 25 Mpc/h with 256^3 dark matter particles plus 256^3 gas elements, h = 0.6711. The parameters of the two codes are not the same physical quantities (A_SN1 is the galactic wind energy per unit star formation in IllustrisTNG and the wind mass loading in SIMBA, and so on); the experiment compares what each code can do with four feedback dials of the same range.

## 3. Observables

At each snapshot, summaries of the galaxy population of the box (every Subfind subhalo with stars; M* is the total bound stellar mass; bins of 0.25 dex in M* from 10^9 to 10^11.5 Msun, about 60 star particles at the lower edge): the number density per dex (`gsmf`), the quenched fraction (specific SFR below 1e-11 per yr; `qf`), the median specific SFR of galaxies with SFR > 0 (`sfms`), the median black hole mass (`bhms`) and the fraction of galaxies with a black hole (`bhocc`), the median SFR-weighted gas metallicity (`mzr`) and stellar metallicity (`zstar`) over 0.0127, the median stellar half-mass radius (`size`), the median gas fraction inside twice the stellar half-mass radius (`fgas`); for centrals, in bins of 0.25 dex in M200c from 10^11 to 10^13, the median M*/M200c (`shmr`), and the halo mass function in bins of 0.5 dex from 10^10.5 to 10^13.5 (`hmf`); and two densities per snapshot (`rho`: stellar mass in subhaloes and the instantaneous SFR, per comoving volume). The exact definitions are in the header of `scripts/ingest_camels_galaxies.py`. A *block* is a family at one snapshot; an *output* is one bin of a block.

**Eligibility.** An output enters if it is finite in at least 90 % of the LH simulations of each code and in at least 20 of the 27 CV simulations of each code. A block enters if it has at least 3 eligible outputs (all of them, for the two densities of `rho`). The `hmf` family is the negative control (section 5), not an observable of the joint distance. Eligibility is reported.

## 4. Estimators

* **Emulator.** For each code and each output, a weighted polynomial ridge regression of the output on the six parameter coordinates x = ((Omega_m - 0.3)/0.2, (sigma_8 - 0.8)/0.2, log A_SN1/log 4, log A_AGN1/log 4, log A_SN2/log 2, log A_AGN2/log 2), as in `PROTOCOL-model-form.md` section 4 (degree 2 or 3 chosen per code by 10-fold cross-validation over all eligible outputs, degree 3 only if its mean error is more than 2 % smaller; ridge 1e-3 of the mean diagonal; Gaussian weights of width 0.5 on the two cosmological coordinates; cosmology fixed at the fiducial values x1 = x2 = 0 everywhere below). Two code-specific facts: the fold assignments of the degree choice and of the validations differ.
* **Noise scale** s_k of an output: s_k^2 = sigma_TNG,k^2 + sigma_SIMBA,k^2 + e_TNG,k^2 + e_SIMBA,k^2, with sigma the standard deviation of the 27 CV simulations and e the cross-validation error of the emulator in excess of the seed noise (zero if smaller), the latter over the simulations within 0.5 of the fiducial cosmology. A floor of 0.02 (in the unit of the output: dex or fraction) is applied to s_k. D = 1 means that the two codes' mean predictions differ, rms over the outputs, by the standard deviation of the difference between one run of each.
* **Weights.** w_k gives every block the same total weight (1 / number of eligible outputs of its block, normalised so that the mean over outputs of w_k is 1 in the set used). A set S of outputs is always a union of blocks.
* **Distance D(S)** as in section 1, minimised over both parameter vectors in [-1, 1]^4 by projected gradient descent with backtracking from the fiducial point and from random starts (the optimiser of `analyze_model_form.py`, tested against brute force there).
* **Matched-size splits and verdict.** In each of 200 random splits both codes' LH sets are divided into two random halves A and B (500 simulations each, the degree chosen on the full sets) and three distances are computed with the same search effort: between = D(TNG A, SIMBA A) and controls D(TNG A, TNG B) and D(SIMBA A, SIMBA B). The margin is m = 1 (the unit of the noise scale). The verdict for a set is *structural* if the 5th percentile of its between distribution exceeds both the 95th percentile of the pooled controls and m; *removable* if its 95th percentile is below m; otherwise *unresolved*. The point estimate on all simulations is reported but does not decide. Distances for families alone and for single snapshots use the first 100 splits.
* **J1** is the verdict for all eligible blocks except `hmf`. **J2** is the verdict for each family alone (all its snapshots). **J3** is the retuning conflict: with D_sep^2 = the sum over families of (blocks of the family / all blocks) x D(family alone)^2 (every family tuned with its own parameter pair), D(all)^2 is never smaller than D_sep^2, and J3 is the ratio D(all)^2 / D_sep^2 and the excess D(all)^2 - D_sep^2, each with the 5th, 50th and 95th percentile over the first 100 splits (the three distances are computed in the same splits). A ratio of 1 means that one parameter pair serves all families as well as separate pairs would; it is interpreted only where D_sep exceeds the 95th percentile of the controls (below that both numbers are at the noise floor and their ratio is not informative). A structural joint verdict is classed as *irreducible* (at least one family is structural alone) or *conflict* (no family is: only the requirement of one parameter pair separates the codes). **J4**: forward selection of families, starting from the empty set and adding at each step the family that gives the largest point estimate of D(S); at each step the verdict of the set from 50 splits; the *separating set* is the first set in that order that is structural. Also D without each family (point estimate and 50 splits). **J5**: the verdict and distance for all families at each snapshot that has at least 3 eligible blocks (no slope is fitted: at most three snapshots qualify at this resolution, see section 8).
* **Response.** For each code and each parameter, the change of each output when that parameter moves from -1 to +1 at the others' fiducial values, in units of s_k (descriptive).

## 5. Validations (must hold for a conclusion to be stated without qualification; failures are reported and the qualification stated)

* **V1 (emulators).** The cross-validation error of the emulator is at most 1.5 times the seed noise for at least 80 % of the eligible outputs of each code.
* **V2 (negative control).** The halo mass function, which depends on cosmology and little on feedback, is reachable: its joint distance over the snapshots at which it is eligible is below m with the verdict *removable* (the pipeline does not produce structural distances where there is none).
* **V3 (units and volume).** The instantaneous SFR density from the catalogues agrees with the time-averaged SFR density of `camels-sfrd.json` interpolated to the snapshot (A1: at z = 0.95 and 2.15, to within 0.1 dex in the median over simulations, for each code).
* **V4 (stellar mass budget).** At z = 0, the stellar mass density in subhaloes divided by the time integral of the logged SFR density (from `camels-sfrd.json`, formed mass) lies in [0.45, 0.75] for the median simulation of each code (the passing range of V1 of `PROTOCOL-model-form.md`).
* **V5 (numerical stability).** Rerunning the distance of J1 with 8 instead of 4 random starts changes it by less than 5 % (A1: the point estimate uses 8 and the check 4).

## 6. Calibration of the estimators on synthetic data

`scripts/power_joint_reachability.py` (committed with the analysis; synthetic data only) builds two-code worlds with known structure and reports the verdicts of J1 to J3: (a) identical structure (all verdicts must be removable); (b) one observable shifted by an amount no parameter can absorb (J2 must flag that observable, J1 must be structural once the shift exceeds the stated multiple of the noise); (c) every family reachable alone but by a different parameter pair for each (J3 must be well above 1 while J2 flags no family); and the dependence of the verdicts on the size of the shift. The tests in `scripts/test_joint_reachability.py` check the same on small worlds.

## 7. Sensitivity analyses (reported, not used to choose)

S1 emulator degree 2 and 3; S2 kernel width 0.35 and 0.8; S3 the emulator error left out of s_k; S4 floor 0.01 and 0.05; S5 margin m = 2; S6 only the snapshots z = 0 and 0.95; S7 without `bhocc` (black hole seeding is a discrete model choice); S8 without the two lowest mass bins (lower resolution); S9 search range cut to 25, 50 and 75 % of the half-width (D only).

## 9. Limitations declared in advance

1. Two codes: the result is about this pair, not about "structural uncertainty" in general.
2. CAMELS boxes are 25 Mpc/h with 256^3 elements: few galaxies above 10^11 Msun, resolution-limited galaxies at the lower end and above z about 3, and all statements are for CAMELS resolution; star formation and black hole growth are resolution-dependent in both codes.
3. The four parameters per code span the factors the CAMELS team chose, not the factors the code authors consider acceptable; the reachable sets depend on that prior. The search reaches the corners of the design where emulators are least constrained, where the emulators' error is larger than the cross-validation error that enters the noise scale (A1 corrects for it and reports the verdicts at a range cut to 75 %); the controls share that search, which keeps the comparison fair, and the shrunken ranges are reported.
4. The distance depends on the choice of observables and their weights (equal weight per block); the margin m = 1 is the scatter between two seeds, a conventional unit and not a physical tolerance. The report gives distances in these units and per family so that other weights can be read off.
5. Medians of small bins are noisy; the noise scale includes that noise but not its non-Gaussian part.
6. Subfind subhaloes are not galaxies observed on the sky; no aperture, selection or survey effect is modelled, which is the point of the comparison and also what keeps it from being an observational test.

## 10. Amendments

### A1 (2026-10-14, before any outcome was computed)

Made after an independent review of this protocol and the analysis code by a separate agent. The review read the code, ran synthetic experiments (two-code worlds with the eligible block layout, heteroscedastic noise, missing values, cubic responses, independent Latin-hypercube designs) and checked inputs of single simulations (raw arrays of LH_0 at z = 0 in both codes, the headers at the five snapshots, the star formation and stellar mass sums of LH_0 to LH_5, the group arrays of LH_0); it computed no family summary of the real ensemble, and neither did the author. The author saw the report, including these input facts: the Subfind commit and field list are the same in both catalogues; the radii definitions agree (mass inside twice the half-mass radius over M* is 0.836 and 0.829, inside the half-mass radius 0.499 and 0.498); the black hole mass is the physical black hole mass in both, the smallest non-zero value is 1.2e6 Msun (TNG, seed 8e5/h) and 2.8e4 Msun (SIMBA), and the black hole occupation among M* > 1e9 is 0.88 and 0.33 at LH_0; TNG carries a wind phase inside its typed masses (median 0.06 % of the gas mass for M* > 1e9) that SIMBA does not; the metal fractions are the sums of the element fractions in both; at LH_0 (different parameters) metallicities are 4 to 5 times lower in SIMBA; the catalogue SFR sum matches the instantaneous log in both and is a median 0.09 dex below the time-averaged bin 0 < z < 0.2 at z = 0 but agrees to 0.03 dex rms at the other snapshots. Everything below was fixed before `python3 scripts/analyze_joint_reachability.py` was run on the real ensemble; the committed code of that commit is the code that is run.

*Emulator error at the minimiser (replaces the verdict statistics of section 4; corrects the claim of section 9, item 3, that the controls share the search).* Cross-validation error is measured near the fiducial cosmology at random parameter points, but the minimiser of the distance sits at the edge of the parameter box, where the emulators' variance is several times larger (in the review: about 0.1 noise units at the fiducial point, 0.3 at interior points, 0.9 at corners for half-sample emulators), and the distance between two noisy emulators is biased upwards by that variance. In each split the variance of a half-sample emulator at a coordinate vector is estimated as half the mean squared difference, in the scaled units of the set, between the two independent half-sample emulators of the same code (A and B) at that vector; the *corrected distance* is the square root of the squared distance minus the variances of the two emulators at the minimiser, with a floor of zero (the same for the two control pairs, at their own minimisers). The verdicts of section 4 are computed on the corrected distributions of the between-code pair and of the pooled controls; the uncorrected ones are reported beside them. The point estimate on all simulations is also reported corrected, with the variance at its minimiser taken as half the median half-sample variance there. For the joint set and for each family alone, the whole split procedure is repeated with the search range cut to 75 % of the half-width, and its verdict is reported (a structural verdict at the full range is not weakened by it; a removable verdict that depends on the corners is).

*J3 (replaces the J3 text of section 4).* The ratio D(all)^2 / D_sep^2 exceeds 1 even for identical codes, because eight parameters absorb noise in sets of a few outputs (1.5 to 2 in the review's synthetic worlds; 1,300 to 1,800 for a true conflict). It is therefore compared with the same ratio for two halves of one code, computed in the same splits: *above the null* if the 5th percentile of the between-code ratios exceeds the 95th percentile of the control ratios (both over the first 100 splits, raw distances). A structural joint verdict is classed *irreducible within the CAMELS ranges* if at least one family is structural alone; *conflict* if every family is removable alone and the ratio is above the null; *unclassified* otherwise. The earlier flag that compared a full-size D_sep with half-sample controls is dropped.

*Validations.* V3 compares the catalogue SFR density with the star formation log interpolated linearly in log to the snapshot, at z = 0.95 and 2.15 (tolerance 0.1 dex, median over simulations, each code); z = 0 (where the log is a bin average over 0 < z < 0.2) and z = 3 are reported without a requirement. V5 compares 4 and 8 random starts (the point estimate uses 8).

*Estimators and sets (statements the text of sections 3 and 4 lacked).* The degree rule is: degree 3 if the mean over the eligible outputs of the ten-fold cross-validation RMSE (fold seed 0) divided by the larger of the seed noise and the floor is less than 0.98 times that of degree 2. The quenched fraction counts galaxies with zero star formation as quenched; the SFR density of `rho` sums all subhaloes, its stellar mass density only subhaloes with stars. Searches use the fiducial start plus 4 random starts in splits, forward selection and V5's low arm, plus 8 in point estimates; at most 150 iterations. Split counts per set: 200 for the joint set, 100 for each family, each snapshot, the halo mass function and the 75 % range sets, 50 for the drop-one and the nested sets of forward selection; the ratio and excess of J3 use the first 100. A point estimate is on the 1000-simulation emulators. The full-size bootstrap of the first version is dropped (it was not implemented). The last nested set of forward selection repeats the joint set with 50 splits and can carry a different verdict; the *separating set* is called the first structural prefix of the forward selection, and if some family is structural alone it is that family and repeats J2. The report gives the share of the squared joint distance carried by each block at the minimising pair, because the joint distance is a root mean over blocks and one block far off can leave it below the margin.

*Sensitivities.* S7 is split: S7a without `bhocc`, S7b without `bhocc` and `bhms` (both are seed-mass quantities); S8 drops the two lowest mass bins of each family that has them and keeps the blocks that keep at least two outputs (the two densities included); S10 without `rho` (it carries a larger weight per output than any other block).

*Calibration (replaces the description of section 6).* `scripts/power_joint_reachability.py` builds worlds with the eligible block layout of section 8 (the 35 joint blocks and 17 control outputs at z = 0, 0.95 and 2.15), heteroscedastic noise, missing values, independent designs and a known truth, computes the *true* distance of each world from the noise-free responses, and reports the verdict frequencies of J1 to J3 against the true distance, for offsets of every output, of one family, and for conflicting responses, with corner minimisers included; its output is `docs/POWER-joint-reachability.md`.

*Limitations added.* The two codes differ in more than the four feedback dials: hydrodynamic solver, star formation algorithm, yields and wind treatment are all part of what is called structural here. Black hole occupation and mass compare seeding prescriptions (TNG seeds at 8e5/h Msun, SIMBA at much lighter masses), a discrete model choice that no dial changes, so the structural verdict of those families is expected by construction; S7a and S7b show the joint result without them. The noise scale is measured at the fiducial parameters from 27 seeds only (the plug-in inverse variance is biased by about 2 % in D), and the cross-validation excess comes from 900-simulation fits while the verdicts use 500-simulation emulators. Outputs saturated at 0 or 1 (occupation, quenched fraction) have a seed scatter below the floor of 0.02 for some bins; S4 shows the dependence on the floor. Verdicts depend on the margin of 1 only through the rule: the corrected distances are reported so that other margins can be read off.
