- Observer layer (`observer-lib.js`, and the same functions in `sim_highline.py`): Gaussian scatter in log stellar mass (constant or mass-dependent) applied to a binned stellar mass function, with the uncertainty bands propagated and the bins that depend on a guessed continuation beyond the curve flagged; a systematic mass offset; a limiting mass and a smooth completeness; a detection floor for specific star formation rate curves (and for per-galaxy counts). None is a calibrated model of any survey. Tests: closed forms (power law, Gaussian), an independent quadrature and an asymptotic series for a Schechter function, number conservation, JavaScript equal to Python on a fixture, and a check that a no-op convolution is caught (`scripts/test-observer.mjs`, `scripts/test_observer.py`).
- Measurement (`docs/OBSERVER-LAYER.md`, `data/analysis/observer-layer.json`, `scripts/analyze_observer_layer.py`): for every simulation-observation pair of galaxy stellar mass functions that the comparison rules call comparable at similar redshift (668 pairs, 119 simulation records, 50 observed), the median offset before and after 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 dex of scatter on the simulation. Of 568 pairs with an observational uncertainty, 440 have an offset larger than it; 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 dex move 3, 13 and 29 of them to smaller and 12, 25 and 41 the other way (the simulations mostly lie above the observations and scatter raises them). This shows how much of an offset a given scatter could absorb, not that any survey has it. Found on the way: the comparison rule for `gsmf` does not check the population, so a quiescent-galaxy mass function passes against an all-galaxy one (76 pairs removed in this measurement).
- `scripts/test_observer_layer.py` fails when the saved measurement is not what the script produces from the current curves: after merging new curves run `python3 scripts/analyze_observer_layer.py` and commit the two files it writes.
