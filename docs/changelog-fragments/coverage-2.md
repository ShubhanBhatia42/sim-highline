- Checked the SPHINX20 records against the public data release and the paper (`scripts/check_sphinx20_release.py`; internal check, nobody outside the project has reviewed it). The H-alpha luminosity function records, re-derived from the release with separate code, agree in every bin of all 10 records (36 bins, largest difference 0.00004 dex, the rounding of the stored value). The reddening coefficient k(H-alpha) = 2.2 that was fitted to the paper's figure is used only in a check, no record value depends on it; fitted snapshot by snapshot with the volume left free it comes out 2.1 or 2.2 at all seven snapshots (rms 0.015 dex over 250 plotted bins, volume factor 0.997 against the 20 cMpc box). The paper says "Kroupa-like" for the IMF; the records had said it gave no sentence on it, and now carry Kroupa (2001) through the definition fills. The halo mass of the stellar-to-halo records (`katz23.sphinx20.shmr.virial.z6`, `z8`) is right: the release column is log10 M_vir in Msun (the paper's Table 1 is reproduced, and its stellar-mass against halo-mass figure is reproduced by the release columns as they are, 1380 of 1380 galaxies; its halo mass function figure in Msun/h is reproduced count for count, 50 of 50 bins, only with the conversion by h), and M_vir with R_vir imply one overdensity (205 times critical at z = 4.64 to 6, 179 at z = 7 to 10). The offset of 1.2 to 1.4 dex comes in part from the sample (the release holds 0 to 15 % of the haloes below log M_vir = 9.7 (Msun) and all above 10.5) and in part from something that is not explained (1.1 to 1.6 dex above ASTRID, CoDa II and THESAN-1 in the bins where the release holds 41 to 100 % of the haloes); the reasons are written in `data/screen-triaged.json`.
- Emission-line luminosity function `elf`, 33 more records (17 to 50): IllustrisTNG H-alpha luminosity functions of TNG50, TNG100 and TNG300 at z = 0.1 to 6 (Hirschmann et al. 2023, read from a vector figure without a text layer; 25 curves, digitized, not rankable; no dust attenuation outside H II regions; points at and below each curve's peak are left out because the paper says the peaks are resolution limits), and H-beta+[OIII] (z = 0.84 to 3.24) and [OII] (z = 1.47 to 4.69) luminosity functions of HiZELS (Khostovan et al. 2015, from the paper's tables; not corrected for dust or AGN, so they never score against the TNG curves; two bins at z = 3.34 have misprinted half widths in the paper's table, noted in the record). The [OIII]+H-beta and [OII] panels of Hirschmann et al. and the observed [OIII] and [OII] functions of Colbert+13, Sobral+15, Mehta+15 and Comparat+16 were not read.
- UV continuum slope, 24 more records (`uvbeta` 28 to 52): FLARES II (Vijayan et al. 2021: weighted median beta against M_1500 with the 16th to 84th percentile band at z = 5 to 10, 6 records) and FLARES V (Wilkins et al. 2023: median beta against far-UV luminosity at z = 10 to 15 for stellar light alone, with nebular emission, and with dust, 18 records). Neither paper gives the wavelength window of beta, so it is recorded as unspecified and these records can never score against records that state one; FLARES V does not say whether its luminosity axis is attenuated. The 39 observed points drawn in the FLARES II panels are at the values of the dataset's own Bouwens et al. records (difference 0.000), and the y axis of that figure had to be calibrated from its major ticks because its labels sit 4.4 pt below them (snapping labels to the nearest tick gives values 0.05 too high). `uvbetam` is unchanged (9 records): FLARES V's mass figure was not read.
