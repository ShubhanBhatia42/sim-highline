# Changelog fragment: resolution-study

Bullets for CHANGELOG.md (Unreleased):

* Added a sixth, smaller pre-registered study of how much published curves of one model change between resolutions, an internal check on the dataset's own records: `docs/PROTOCOL-resolution.md` (committed before any shift between resolutions was computed), `scripts/analyze_resolution.py`, `scripts/test_resolution.py` (synthetic ladders with known shifts; three deliberately broken versions of the code are caught), the generated report `docs/ANALYSIS-resolution.md` and `data/analysis/resolution.json`, and a study page (`site/src/study-resolution.html`, `scripts/site-resolution.mjs`) with a row in the studies index. The registered studies, their code, tests and outputs are unchanged; the study calls the joint-reachability noise functions unchanged and reads one of its saved results.
* The study built 332 pairs of curves of the same model at two resolutions with equal definitions and a comparable epoch (132 steps in 17 sets of runs). Five sets of runs have a box stated to be the same (the IllustrisTNG and Illustris models at L25n128, L25n256, L25n512; COLIBRE L025 at m7, m6, m5; FLAMINGO L1 at m10, m9, m8; THESAN-2 and THESAN-1): 30 steps between adjacent resolutions, of which the rule fixed in advance labels 2 converged and 28 resolution-dependent (one noise unit is the CAMELS scatter between one run of each code, typically 0.07 dex, a strict margin; at twice the margin 12 are converged). For 19 steps with a counterpart in the joint-reachability study, 16 change by at least the TNG-SIMBA distance of that observable. Another 63 pairs of COLIBRE in other boxes are read from the public data file of its paper (supplementary; nothing added to `data/curves`).
* Checks made after the run (`scripts/check_resolution.py`, `docs/CHECKS-resolution.md`, `data/analysis/resolution-checks.json`), labelled as such: where in each curve the change sits (for the mass functions, mostly the steep high-mass end) and what a vertical change of a mass function amounts to in mass.
* What is missing: CAMELS is not run at a second resolution; SIMBA has no evaluable pair (its fits at two resolutions are parametric); IllustrisTNG, EAGLE and Illustris data pages need an account, the FLAMINGO page shows no credential-free galaxy catalogue and the THESAN page states nothing about data access, so none was used; only the COLIBRE paper file was usable. Most curves stop at the authors' resolution limits, so changes below them are not in the data. Different papers use different galaxy finders and apertures; each pair compares two curves of one paper.
* `scripts/make_registration_trail.py` lists the new study, `data/registration-trail.json` gains its entry (protocol commit before the result commit; `scripts/test_registration.py` passes), `docs/README.md` lists the study, its generated pages and this folder of fragments, and `docs/RESOLUTION.md` has a short pointer to the study.
