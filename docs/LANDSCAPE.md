# What a researcher does today for this job, and where we stand

Written 2026-10-16. The first part is what the project's own source list shows; the second is from web searches and is not verified beyond a search summary.

## From the project's own sources

A researcher who wants "what do simulations predict for this relation at this epoch" today does one of these:

1. **Reads the papers** of each simulation and reads values off figures. Most of this dataset's curves were produced that way, which is why 54 % are digitized and carry an error budget.
2. **Uses the simulation teams' own comparison data.** Some teams publish tables with their curves and the observations they compare to (for example the SWIFT/VELOCIraptor comparison collection and the COLIBRE release, both ingested here with their hashes). These are accurate for that one simulation but each has its own definitions and layout.
3. **Downloads the catalogues** (IllustrisTNG, EAGLE, CAMELS and others) and measures the relation themselves with their own choices. This is the most accurate and the slowest, and it produces curves that differ from the published ones by exactly the definitions this project records.

## From searches (not verified)

- No web tool that overlays published curves from many simulations with a check of whether they are comparable turned up in two searches. That could mean none exists or that the search was too narrow.
- Research groups compare codes on set problems (the AGORA project for zoom-in galaxies) and CAMELS runs many codes over a shared parameter box. Neither is a lookup of published relations.

## Where we are better

- One place, one axis convention, with the definitions beside every curve.
- A fairness check on every comparison, including your own curve.
- The dials page, which lets a student feel what feedback does.

## Where we are worse

- Nothing here is as accurate as measuring the relation yourself from the catalogue.
- 54 % of the simulation curves are digitized and have not been checked by anyone who knows the simulation.
- Coverage is uneven: some relations have a handful of curves.
- It has no users yet, so we do not know what researchers actually ask for.
