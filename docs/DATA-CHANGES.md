# What has been changed in the data, and what has not

Generated by `scripts/audit_conversions.py`; do not edit by hand. The numbers are counted from the records themselves and from the conversion scripts of the one compilation we ingest.

## The short answer

**The simulation curves are stored as their authors published them.** Nothing has been corrected for the initial mass function (IMF), the stellar-mass aperture, which galaxies are counted, dust or the cosmology: where two curves differ in those, the difference is recorded and flagged, not removed. What we did change is only the way numbers are written (sections 1 and 2) and the fixes in section 5; the observation curves that came through a compilation carry that compilation's own conversions (section 3), except where we checked them against the papers and corrected them (section 3 and [COMPILATION-CHECK.md](COMPILATION-CHECK.md)). Each change is also written in the warning of every record it touches.

## 2. The one cosmology conversion we made ourselves

17 curves (Magneticum Pathfinder): the released mass functions are densities in units that carry h, converted with h = 0.704 (the simulation's own value, so the curve stays in the run's own cosmology and is not moved to another one).

## 3. What the compilation we ingest did, before we received the numbers

173 observation curves come from the SWIFT/VELOCIraptor comparison-data compilation ([calvin-sykes/velociraptor-comparison-data](https://github.com/calvin-sykes/velociraptor-comparison-data), commit `fc740f4`). Its scripts re-express each published table in its own conventions. From reading those scripts:

- 142 of the 173 are corrected for h to a Planck15 cosmology (the observers' own h is replaced by the compilation's).
- 9 of them also have their stellar masses converted to a Chabrier IMF by the compilation (Kroupa to Chabrier: mass times 0.912, -0.040 dex; Salpeter to Chabrier: times 0.606, -0.218 dex): Andrews2013 (from Kroupa, 1 curve); Gallazzi2005 (from Kroupa, 2 curves); Grazian2015 (from Salpeter, 4 curves); Kirby2013 (from Kroupa, 1 curve); Reyes2011 (from Kroupa, 1 curve).
- 15 are taken as published and 16 come from a file whose script could not be matched.

The counts above come from the constants each script defines, which is not enough: reading the scripts of the curves below showed three that define h and never use it (Andrews 2013, Reyes 2011, Kirby 2013), one whose comment claims an IMF conversion it does not apply (Gonzalez 2011) and one that applies a literal factor the count cannot see (Muzzin 2013). Where the compilation's conversion had been taken on trust, **50 of these curves were checked against the original papers** (the paper's own printed numbers or figure, after the paper's own h and IMF): 24 agree, 3 agree with the fit the paper prints, 20 disagree and 3 cannot be checked. Of the disagreements, 8 records had their numbers shifted by a stated amount, 16 more had a label corrected (cosmology, population, velocity definition, tier or rankability) and 3 carry a note with the numbers unchanged; each is listed with its evidence in `data/compilation-corrections.json` and in [COMPILATION-CHECK.md](COMPILATION-CHECK.md). The checks were made by one reader; no author or outside expert has reviewed them, and the remaining curves have not been checked. To look at one, the record's `provenance.sourceMember` names the compilation file and its warning quotes the compilation's own note.

## 4. An optional switch on screen

The Explorer can shift the stellar-mass and SFR axes of simulation curves to a Chabrier IMF (Kroupa -0.040 dex, Salpeter -0.217 dex). It is off by default, labelled in the legend, the evidence panel and exported figures when on, never applied to curves whose IMF is not stated, and **downloads and the Python loader always hold the published values**. These offsets are population averages (uncertain at about 0.02 dex) and are the same factors the compilation applied in section 3 (data/imf-offsets.json; the Explorer used slightly different, undocumented values until 2026-10-18, and the two registered studies keep the offsets they were registered with).

## 5. Mistakes we fixed

14 fixes to data, analyses or claims are listed, with the release each was made in, in [CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md).

## 5b. Where the numbers come from when it is not the cited paper

For 101 records the numbers do not simply come from the paper the record cites: 37 numbers from an author's web page, through the compilation; 20 a release that lists several papers, with the quantity's own paper identified; 20 computed from the simulation's catalogue by the compilation's maintainers, not the cited paper's curve; 18 a curve reproduced from a paper other than the one cited; 3 evaluated from a published fit by the compilation's software; 2 values taken from a second paper; 1 read from a figure by the compilation. Each says so in its `provenance.attribution` (with the sentence, the evidence and whether it was stated or inferred), the records table has `attribution_kind` and `primary_source` columns, the BibTeX file credits the primary source as well as the cited paper, and the Evidence panel shows a "numbers from" line. The other records were read as plotting the cited paper's own curve (834 were checked against the paper's figure caption or text; 469 were not individually checked). Twelve compilation records carry a citation from the compilation's files that names a different paper from the one given here; their primary source is undecided (see COMPILATION-CHECK.md).

## 6. Definitions added from the papers

459 simulation records carry at least one definition (for example the stellar-mass aperture) that the table or figure left unstated and the source paper states (60 sourced rules in `data/definition-fills.json`, each with paper, section, a short quote and the SHA-256 of the arXiv source read). A stated value is never overwritten. 23 papers were read for a definition and do not state it (listed on the Methods page); those values stay unspecified.

## What the analyses do with this

- The harmonisation study applies IMF offsets and aperture maps inside its own calculation, to test whether matching definitions closes the gap between simulated mass functions. It changes no stored curve.
- The definition-budget analysis measures how large definition differences are. It corrects no curve.
- The dials page uses the CAMELS simulations as measured (every CAMELS code is measured the same way, so no conversion is needed between them). The published and observed curves drawn on it are not adjusted to the CAMELS definitions; the page says so.
- Check your run does not convert your numbers. It tells you which of your definitions differ from each curve's.
