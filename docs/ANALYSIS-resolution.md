# Resolution: how much a model's published output changes with it

Generated by `scripts/analyze_resolution.py` from release 0.24.0; do not edit by hand. The pre-specified protocol is [`PROTOCOL-resolution.md`](PROTOCOL-resolution.md) (committed before any shift was computed); this page reports what it gave. Internal check: no outside expert has reviewed it. It compares published curves of one model at two resolutions, taken from the dataset's own records; it does not test CAMELS, IllustrisTNG or SIMBA at a second resolution.

## 1. Answers

* **Pairs.** 332 epoch-pairs of the same model at two resolutions with equal definitions and a comparable epoch could be built from the dataset, in 132 units (ladder, step, relation) in 17 ladders; 30 adjacent-step units in 5 ladders (five were registered) have a box stated to be the same (tier A), where the shift can be read as resolution. Another 63 epoch-pairs of COLIBRE in other boxes come from the public file (supplementary).
* **P1.** Of the 30 tier A adjacent-step units, 2 are converged (shift below one noise unit), 28 resolution-dependent and 0 cannot be told; 19 rest on the CAMELS noise scale, the others on the typical-noise yardstick.
* **P2.** For the 19 units with a counterpart in the joint-reachability study, the resolution shift is at least as large as the TNG-SIMBA distance of that observable in 16; the median ratio of shift to distance is 2.09.

A noise unit is the standard deviation of the difference between one run of each of the two CAMELS codes (the unit of the joint-reachability distances). R is the root-mean-square shift between the two resolutions in that unit, R_c the same with the digitization noise removed in quadrature. Dex is a factor of 10 in the quantity (0.1 dex is a factor of 1.26); the quenched fraction is a fraction, and its shift is in fraction. The columns 'rms shift', 'mean shift' and 'largest' are in dex (or fraction), finer minus coarser.

## 12. Expectations written in advance

Protocol section 8 recorded what was expected before the run. Counts, from the numbers above:

* Expected: steps whose parameters the authors recalibrated are close. By the rule, 2 of 24 such steps are converged.
* Expected: the code distance exceeds most resolution shifts where the joint study's retuned distance is large, and not where it is small. Of the 2 comparisons with a retuned distance of at least 1 noise unit, 1 have a resolution shift at least as large as the compared distance; of the 17 with a smaller distance, 15.
* Expected: star formation histories change more at high redshift than at low redshift. Not tested separately.

## 13. Deviations from the protocol

* The wording and layout of the report were revised after the run (unit names with the pipe character broke one table; a variant column was added to the comparison table; a section setting the expectations of the protocol against the counts was added); no number was recomputed, and the first output is in the version history.

## 14. What this cannot say

* It is not a convergence test of CAMELS: CAMELS is not run at a second resolution here, and SIMBA has no evaluable pair in the dataset. The shifts are those of other models, from their papers.
* Each pair compares two curves of one paper, so the galaxy finder, aperture and mass definition are that paper's. Different papers use different ones; nothing here compares across papers, and the noise unit from CAMELS Subfind catalogues is only a yardstick for them.
* Most curves keep only what lies above the authors' resolution limit, so the shifts below that limit, where resolution matters most, are not in the data and the numbers here understate the effect there.
* Where the authors recalibrate at each resolution, a small shift reflects that recalibration.
* Epochs and relations within a ladder are correlated; the counts are counts of reported rows, not independent tests.
