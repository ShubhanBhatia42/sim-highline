Limits in brief. This is an internal check: no outside expert has reviewed it. It compares published curves of other models; CAMELS itself is not run at a second resolution, so it does not say what IllustrisTNG or SIMBA would give at CAMELS or at a higher resolution. Different papers use different galaxy finders and apertures; each pair compares two curves of one paper only.

§1Question

The joint study and the shape study both name resolution as their largest open weakness: CAMELS boxes are 25 Mpc/h with 256³ dark matter and 256³ gas elements, and nothing in the project measured how much a model's output changes when only the resolution changes. CAMELS has no second resolution, so that cannot be measured on CAMELS. What can be measured is how published curves of other models change between resolutions of the same model, and how large that is next to two numbers the project already has: the run-to-run noise of the CAMELS observables, and the distance between IllustrisTNG and SIMBA that the joint study reports.

Two questions with rules fixed in advance:

  • How many steps are converged? For each step between adjacent resolutions of one model in a box stated to be the same, the root-mean-square change of the curve is measured in noise units (one unit is the scatter between one run of each CAMELS code). Below one unit: converged. With the digitization noise taken out, above one unit: resolution-dependent. Otherwise: cannot tell.
  • Is the change smaller or larger than the gap between the codes? The same change, in the same units, set against the IllustrisTNG–SIMBA distance for the same kind of observable.

§4Reading it

All tier A steps, with the span from the coarsest to the finest run where there are three. R is the root-mean-square change in noise units, Rc the same with the digitization noise taken out; the last three columns are in dex (a factor of 10; 0.1 dex is a factor of 1.26), or in fraction for the quenched fraction, finer minus coarser. "Noise basis" says whether the unit is the CAMELS noise of the matching observable and mass bins (reference) or a typical CAMELS noise (yardstick), used where the observable has no counterpart or too few mass bins overlap.

§5Limits and gaps

  • Internal check. No outside expert has reviewed the protocol, the code or the result. The labels are not significance tests: the only uncertainty in R is the digitization of the figures.
  • CAMELS is not tested at a second resolution. The changes are those of other models, from their papers. SIMBA has no pair that can be evaluated in the dataset (its published fits at two resolutions are parametric and were not evaluated), so nothing here measures the resolution dependence of SIMBA.
  • Different papers, finders and apertures. Each pair compares two curves of one paper. The noise unit comes from CAMELS Subfind catalogues with total bound masses and is only a yardstick for curves with other definitions.
  • The curves stop at the authors' resolution limits. Most records keep only what lies above a resolution limit, so the changes below it, where resolution matters most, are not in the data. The numbers understate the effect there.
  • Recalibration. COLIBRE and FLAMINGO recalibrate their subgrid parameters at each resolution, so a small change there reflects that recalibration. Only the two Pillepich et al. sets of runs change nothing but the resolution, and they cover one observable, the cosmic star formation rate density.
  • One margin. A noise unit is a convention, not a physical tolerance. It is small (typically {{rsTypical}} dex), so the labels are strict; the counts at other margins are given above.
  • Few independent sets of runs. Epochs and observables within one set of runs are correlated. The counts are counts of rows, not independent tests, and no p-value is given.
