Do galaxy simulations agree with each other, and with the sky?

sim-highline puts every published prediction for a galaxy scaling relation on one axis, next to the observations, and keeps the definitions that decide whether two curves can be compared. {{records}} curves, {{suites}} simulations, {{obsSources}} observational sources, {{relations}} relations, z = 0 to {{zMax}}.

Four places to look

Early galaxies: the stellar mass function at z ≈ 6

How many galaxies of a given mass exist a billion years after the Big Bang tests star-formation efficiency and feedback directly, and it is where JWST now provides data.

Cosmic noon: the star-forming main sequence at z ≈ 2

The tight link between star-formation rate and stellar mass sets how fast galaxies grow. Published simulations have long been reported to differ from observations in normalisation here, and from one another, by amounts comparable to how star-forming galaxies and SFRs are defined.

Metals: the gas-phase mass–metallicity relation at z ≈ 0

Winds remove metals most efficiently from low-mass galaxies, so the relation records feedback. Its absolute scale also depends on the abundance calibration used by the observers, which can matter as much as the physics.

Black holes: black-hole mass against stellar mass at z ≈ 0

Seeding, supernova and AGN feedback all leave a mark here. Published comparisons find tight relations in every simulation but differences in shape, normalisation and the number of low-mass black holes in massive galaxies.

How to read it

Evidence tierOfficial table, catalogue, paper table, published fit, or digitized from a figure. Digitized curves are never used for ranking.
Source badgeA letter on every legend row says where the data come from; hover for the paper. Dotted lines are digitized figures.
DefinitionsMass aperture, IMF, population, SFR timescale and more are stored per curve; ✓ / ~ / ✕ marks whether two curves are comparable.
No interpolationA simulation with no output near your redshift simply disappears; nothing is filled in.

What you can do

  • Compare. Step through redshift, switch simulations on and off, pick a reference, read residuals.
  • Understand. A short note on every relation: what observations show, where simulations agree and differ, what it means physically, with reviews linked.
  • Download. Exactly what you see, or everything, as tidy tables for pandas; BibTeX; figures with a citation footer.

What it is not

  • Not a replacement for the original data. {{digitizedShare}} of simulation curves are digitized from published figures; only {{rankableShare}} are marked rankable.
  • Not a leaderboard. Many curves cannot be compared like for like, and sim-highline says so.
  • Not independently verified yet. {{auditNote}}
  • Not complete. Some relations have only three simulations so far (total gas fraction, stellar Tully–Fisher, HI and H2 fractions, black hole mass–dispersion); UV luminosity functions ({{nUvlf}} simulations) and the cosmic SFR density ({{nSfrd}}) are recent additions.
