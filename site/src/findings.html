How much of the disagreement between simulations is just definitions?

Two simulations that plot the same relation often measure different things: a 30 kpc or a 50 kpc stellar mass, a Salpeter or a Chabrier IMF, a 10 or a 100 Myr star formation rate. sim-highline keeps each curve's definitions and asks, without correcting a single curve, what the published record can and cannot support.

The honest conclusion is a limit, not a headline: the binding constraint on comparing simulations is not the physics but whether each source says what it measured.

What the documented definitions allow

Every pair of curves from different simulations, for seven relations at five epochs, is classified from the documented definitions alone: matched (all keys stated and equal), differing (a stated difference, for example 30 versus 50 kpc), unknown (a definition is missing in one of them) or not comparable (a defining quantity differs, for example two different size definitions).

What one definition does to a curve

The cleanest measurement of a definition is inside a single run that is published under two of them. Nothing else differs, so the offset is purely definitional. {{fSensN}} such pairs form {{fSensG}} groups; population splits (active versus passive) and model variations are excluded.

Reading it. Stellar-mass apertures move the mass function by a few hundredths of a dex on average but by up to several tenths at the massive end; a halo-mass definition moves the stellar-to-halo relation by 0.02 to 0.10 dex; an SFR timescale moves the main sequence by 0.1 to 0.4 dex at high redshift; cold-gas density thresholds move the gas fraction by 0.3 to 0.6 dex. None of these is a physics difference.

A result that went away

Before the definitions were completed from the papers, one stellar-mass-function epoch (z about 6) looked significantly better matched for definition-matched pairs (p = 0.004). After {{fRecRecords}} records were completed it did not (p about 0.1): the apparent detection was produced by undocumented fields. Of {{fTests}} epoch tests, {{fBelow}} fall below p = 0.05 where {{fExpect}} would be expected by chance, and {{fBonf}} survive Bonferroni correction.

Can documented definitions close the gap in the z ≈ 0 mass function?

A pre-specified test, written before the numbers existed: express each of {{hSuites}} simulation suites' z ≈ 0 stellar mass function in one IMF, without added mass scatter and in a 30 pkpc aperture, using only documented definitions and aperture maps measured inside single runs, and ask what fraction R of the spread across suites (in the stellar mass at fixed abundance) disappears. The rule: the bootstrap interval of R must exclude 0, a label-permutation test must give p < 0.05, and the sign must agree in two sensitivity analyses.

Parametric or structural? What retuning a simulation can and cannot change

Simulations are tuned to a few observations at redshift about 0 and then asked what happens elsewhere. Two things limit those predictions: what a model can still do by retuning its own parameters (parametric), and what its form forces whatever the tuning (structural). Climate science separates them with perturbed-physics and multi-model ensembles. Here the cosmic star formation history is the test case, with a pre-specified protocol written before any of these numbers existed (and amended once, before the run, after an independent review): published curves of nine model families, the authors' own resolution, parameter and cosmology variants, and the CAMELS project's thousand-simulation experiments with the IllustrisTNG and SIMBA codes at identical resolution.

Limits

  • Digitized records are included. About half of the simulation curves are digitized from figures and are never rankable; the rankable-only run in the data is the robustness check and is much smaller.
  • Definition labels are coarse. “Unspecified” hides everything, and two labels that read alike may differ in detail.
  • Within-run pairs are few. The sensitivity catalogue covers {{fSensG}} groups; absence of a measurement is not absence of an effect.
  • No expert has verified these records yet. A single author confirming a suite's definitions would change the status of its curves.
