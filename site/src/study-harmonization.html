§1Question

Do the documented differences in IMF, mass scatter and stellar-mass aperture explain the spread between simulated z ≈ 0 stellar mass functions?

The definitions study asks a similar question and cannot answer it: it reuses each curve in many pairs, has few matched pairs per test, and {{fBonf}} of {{fTests}} tests survive correction. This study replaces it with one statistic on one relation at one epoch, specified before it was computed.

§2Data and rule

Each of {{hSuites}} simulation suites’ z ≈ 0 stellar mass function is expressed in one IMF, without added mass scatter and in a 30 pkpc aperture. Only documented definitions and aperture maps measured inside single runs are used. The coordinate is the stellar mass at fixed abundance. R is the fraction of the spread across suites that disappears after harmonizing.

Decision rule

The bootstrap interval of R must exclude 0, a label-permutation test must give p < 0.05, and the sign must agree in two sensitivity analyses. All three, or no detection.

The rule is fixed before the statistic exists. The protocol records what had been looked at when it was written, and each amendment is dated with what had been seen at the time.

§3Result

Reading R

R = 1: the spread across suites disappears. R = 0: harmonizing changes nothing. R < 0: it increases the spread.

Result: {{hVerdict}}. R = {{hR}}, 95% interval {{hCI}}, permutation p = {{hP}}.

§4Limits

  • Power. {{hPower}}
  • Sampling noise is not modelled. Few galaxies per bin in the tail inflate the dispersion before and after harmonization alike and lower the ceiling on R; a sensitivity analysis in the analysis document addresses it.
  • One relation, one epoch. The result says nothing about other relations or redshifts.
  • Documented definitions only. Where a suite does not state a definition, no value is assumed (Methods, limits).

The pre-specification, every amendment with what had been seen when it was made, the sensitivity analyses and the deviations are in ANALYSIS-harmonization.md.
