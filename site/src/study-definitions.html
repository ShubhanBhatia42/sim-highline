Study · Definitions

How much of the disagreement between simulations is just definitions?

Abstract

Two simulations that plot the same relation often measure different things: a 30 or a 50 kpc stellar mass, a Salpeter or a Chabrier IMF, a 10 or a 100 Myr star formation rate. Without correcting any curve, {{fPairs}} cross-simulation curve pairs are classified by what their documented definitions allow, and the shift caused by one definition is measured inside single runs. {{fMatchedPct}} of pairs have every needed definition documented and equal; {{fUnknownPct}} cannot be classified because a source does not say what it measured. The binding constraint on comparing simulations is documentation, not physics.

§1Question

How much of the disagreement between simulations at fixed stellar mass (or halo mass) can be attributed to differences in documented definitions, as opposed to physics or numerics? No curve is corrected: pairs of records from different simulations are classified from their documented definitions alone, and their offsets are compared.

The cleanest measurement of a definition is inside a single run that is published under two of them. Nothing else differs there, so the offset is purely definitional.

§2Data

Documentation

{{fUndoc}} definition values remain open. The list, with the paper to read for each, is UNDOCUMENTED-DEFINITIONS.md.

Every pair of curves from different simulations, for seven relations at five epochs, is classified from the documented definitions alone:

matched
every needed key is stated and equal
differing
a stated difference, for example 30 versus 50 kpc
unknown
a definition is missing in one of the two records
not comparable
a defining quantity differs, for example two different size definitions

{{fRecRecords}} of {{fSimRecords}} simulation records had definitions recovered from the source papers, each with section, quote and the checksum of the archive read (evidence ledger). Inside single runs, {{fSensN}} pairs form {{fSensG}} groups; population splits (active versus passive) and model variations are excluded.

A result that went away

Before the definitions were completed from the papers, one stellar-mass-function epoch (z about 6) looked significantly better matched for definition-matched pairs (p = 0.004, recorded in the changelog for 0.17.0). After {{fRecRecords}} records were completed it did not (p = {{fP6}}): the apparent detection was produced by undocumented fields. Of {{fTests}} epoch tests, {{fBelow}} fall below p = 0.05 where {{fExpect}} would be expected by chance, and {{fBonf}} survive Bonferroni correction. The data neither show nor exclude a definitional contribution.

§4Limits

  • Digitized records are included. {{digitizedShare}} of the simulation curves are digitized from figures and are never rankable; the rankable-only run in the data is the robustness check and is much smaller.
  • Definition labels are coarse. “Unspecified” hides everything, and two labels that read alike may differ in detail.
  • Within-run pairs are few. The sensitivity catalogue covers {{fSensG}} groups; absence of a measurement is not absence of an effect.
  • Descriptive. There is no protocol; each curve is reused in many pairs, so the tests are not independent. The mass-function study asks a narrower question with a single pre-specified statistic.
  • Not independently verified. No expert has checked these records yet; a single author confirming a suite’s definitions would change the status of its curves.
