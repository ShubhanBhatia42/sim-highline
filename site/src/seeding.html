How the two codes plant their black holes, and why it matters for the result

In plain words

{{plainSeedOne}}

{{plainSeed}}

What this page is. An explanation of one internal result of this project (the all-at-once study). No outside expert has reviewed the result or this page. It does not claim that the result, or the explanation, is new: the CAMELS papers already report that the two codes differ, and the seeding rules below are the model authors' own. Every statement about how the codes seed black holes is a quotation from a paper or from a file of this repository, with its place; a statement that is in none of them is left out.

§1What seeding means

A galaxy simulation follows gas, stars and dark matter. The two models here also include central black holes. In both, the code places a black hole particle into the simulation by a rule, once a halo or a galaxy passes a size threshold. That step is called seeding. Here is how the authors of each model describe it.

{{seedQTngRule}} {{seedQSimbaRule}}

§4What the study found

The study asked whether retuning each code's four dials can make the two agree on galaxy properties. Distances are in noise units: one unit is the scatter between one run of each code. Details are on the study page.

  • All properties at once. The codes stay {{seedJoint}} noise units apart (half-sample range {{seedJointLo}} to {{seedJointHi}}), far above the margin of 1.
  • Black hole occupation is the largest single piece. Fitted alone, the two codes stay {{seedOccAlone}} apart at the best dial setting found, against {{seedOccFid}} at the default dials ({{seedOccAloneRaw}} before the emulators' own error is removed). Its blocks carry {{seedOccShare}} of the squared all-at-once distance. {{seedOccNative}}
  • It is not the whole story. Without black hole occupation the all-at-once distance is {{seedNoOcc}}; without occupation and black hole mass it is {{seedNoBoth}}. Both stay above 1 (point estimates on all simulations).
  • Black hole mass among galaxies that have one can be matched. Alone it is {{seedMassAlone}}, below the margin. The dials can set the typical mass of a black hole; they cannot make the fraction of galaxies that hold one agree.
  • Gas metallicity also stays apart, by {{seedMzrAlone}} when fitted alone and with {{seedMzrShare}} of the all-at-once squared distance. It is not one of the two properties that compare seeding rules, and no quotation here explains it.
{{seedFamRows}}
PropertyFitted aloneDistance aloneShare of all-at-onceCompares seeding rules

Distance alone: the best the dials of both codes can do for that property, in noise units, with the emulators' own error removed. Share: the part of the squared all-at-once distance that comes from the property's blocks at the best joint setting. "Compares seeding rules" is the protocol's own statement quoted in §2, not a result.

§5What the study could not show

  • That seeding causes the difference. No run changed a seeding rule. The experiment varies four feedback parameters and, in the words quoted in §2, does not vary other baryonic processes. The protocol records that a structural verdict for the two properties that compare seeding rules is "expected by construction", so the result for them says little beyond the fact that the rules differ. The analysis file adds that the codes also differ in other ways:
{{seedQMore}}
  • How much of the {{seedJoint}} would remain with the same rule in both codes. Leaving the occupation output out ({{seedNoOcc}}) is not the same as running both codes with one rule. Nobody has done that run here.
  • Whether the difference is physical or comes from resolution. The experiment is at one resolution (25 Mpc/h boxes with 256³ particles of each kind). The repository's resolution note says:
{{seedQRes}}
  • Dropping the two lowest stellar-mass bins of every property takes the all-at-once distance from {{seedJoint}} to {{seedNoLow}}. Those are the two bins where SIMBA's occupation is {{seedLowSimba}} and {{seedLow2Simba}}, but the same cut applies to all properties, so the study does not say how much of the drop is occupation. No run at another resolution exists to test it.
  • Anything about real black holes. The comparison is between two simulations, deliberately without observations. Neither rule is shown to be more realistic than the other.
  • Anything about other codes. The third-code check above is exploratory, uses three codes from the one CAMELS project, and does not describe Astrid's rule.
  • That the sources describe the runs exactly. The quotations are from the papers' descriptions of the models. They were not checked for this page against the configuration files of the CAMELS runs.
  • Review. The registered protocol was reviewed internally only. No outside expert has reviewed the result, the quotations' use here, or this page.
