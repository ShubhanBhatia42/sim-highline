The problem it removes

Comparing what different simulations predict means opening a dozen papers, reading values off figures, and guessing whether each uses the same stellar-mass aperture, IMF, population and cosmology. Those choices move curves by tenths of a dex, as much as the physics you are trying to compare. sim-highline records them for every curve and says when a comparison is not like with like.

What you get

Compare

One relation, one epoch, every simulation. Step through redshift, show each simulation's fiducial run or all its variants, pick a reference and see residuals, and read a ✓ / ~ / ✕ for whether two curves share definitions.

Understand

Each relation has a short summary of how the literature describes it, what simulations and observations agree and disagree on, and what it means physically, with past and present reviews linked.

Download

Exactly what you see, or all epochs, as tidy CSV or JSON with units, definitions, evidence tier and citation on every row; BibTeX; and sim_highline.py to load everything into pandas.

Trace

Every curve opens to its source, how it was extracted, and a checksum of the file it came from. Nothing is interpolated: a simulation with no data at your redshift simply disappears.

How it stays honest

  • Evidence tiers on every curve. Official tables, catalogue-derived, tabulated in a paper, published fit, or digitized from a figure. Digitized curves are never used for ranking.
  • Exact extraction. Figures are read from the vector paths of the paper's own source files, not traced from pixels, and every extraction script regenerates its records byte for byte.
  • Automatic checks. {{auditNote}}
  • Definitions travel with the data. Mass aperture, halo definition, IMF, cosmology, population, SFR timescale and the like are structured fields, so the comparison flags are computed, not asserted.

What it is not

  • Not a replacement for the original data. {{digitizedShare}} of simulation curves are digitized from published figures. Where an author releases a table, use that.
  • Not a leaderboard. Many curves cannot be compared like for like, and sim-highline says so instead of scoring them.
  • Not independently verified yet. It is a beta from one maintainer; a sample of curves is waiting for review by people who know each simulation.
  • Not complete. Some relations are thin: stellar Tully–Fisher, total gas fraction, HI and H2 fractions and the black hole mass–dispersion relation have only three simulations each. UV luminosity functions ({{nUvlf}} simulations) and the cosmic SFR density ({{nSfrd}}) are recent additions.
