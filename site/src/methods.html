Methods

Methods

How a number gets into sim-highline, and what it is allowed to mean. The long form is docs/METHODS.md.

§1How values enter

Evidence enters through four routes, and every record carries the tier that says which. Counts per tier are on the Data page.

Official tables
Tables released by a collaboration (for example THESAN-1 relation files, Magneticum Box4/uhr tables, the COSMOS-Web mass function). Tier official-table; the SHA-256 of the file is stored.
Papers
Fits and tables transcribed from the papers. Tiers published-table (spot-checked against numbers the paper quotes) and published-fit (a fitting function evaluated, not refitted). Transcribed values are never labelled official-table.
Compilations
Machine-readable compilations, pinned to a commit: the SWIFT/VELOCIraptor observational comparison collection and the Sharda et al. 2026 COLIBRE mass–metallicity data release.
Catalogues
sim-highline-derived binning of individual-galaxy tables by script, with fixed cuts, bootstrap errors and a fixed seed. Tier catalog-derived.

Where none of these exists, a curve is read from a published figure (§2): tier digitized-figure, never rankable.

§2Exact extraction from vector figures

{{digitizedShare}} of simulation curves are digitized. No raster tracing is used. Figures are taken from the arXiv source of each paper, which almost always holds the figure as vector PDF, EPS or PS. The curves, markers and fill polygons are read from the drawing commands, so values are exact to the coordinate precision of the file.

  • Axes are calibrated from the labelled ticks of the same figure: a robust fit over numeric labels snapped to ticks for linear axes; decade labels matched to major ticks and validated against the minor-tick pattern for log axes; calibration translated between shared-axis panels.
  • Assertions. Every digitizer asserts its calibration and, where the paper quotes numbers, spot-checks against them. A record never contains a point the figure does not draw; bands come from fill polygons, and points with no drawn error bar have no band.
  • Regeneration. Digitizers are deterministic: the shipped records are regenerated exactly by python3 scripts/regress_digitizers.py. The stored checksum is that of the original figure file.

§3Definitions travel with the data

Mass aperture, halo definition, IMF, cosmology, population, SFR timescale and statistic, quenching criterion, size definition, metallicity tracer and the like are structured fields on every record, so the comparison flags are computed, not asserted. For each relation, compare.js lists the keys that must match (hard) and those that only warrant a caveat (soft). A pair of records is then one of:

✓ comparable
no blocker and no caveat: every hard and soft key documented and equal
~ with caveats
hard keys documented and equal; a soft key differs or is undocumented, or the two epochs are more than 0.5 apart in z
✕ not comparable
a hard key differs or is undocumented, a curve is marked context only, or structured definitions are missing
Not converted

IMF and cosmology are stored, not converted, for every simulation curve (the one exception is an h-unit conversion, §4). The Explorer can shift stellar masses to a Chabrier IMF on screen, labelled; downloads always hold the published values. Everything we did change, and what the observation compilation had already changed, is in §4.

Some tables and figures do not state a definition that the source paper does. Each such value is recovered with the paper, section, a short quote and the SHA-256 of the arXiv source archive (§8); a definition that cannot be quoted stays unspecified. A fill never overwrites a stated value.

§4What we changed in the data, and what we did not

The simulation curves are stored as their authors published them. Nothing is corrected for the initial mass function (IMF), the stellar-mass aperture, which galaxies are counted, dust or the cosmology. Where two curves differ in those, the difference is recorded and flagged, not removed. What we did change is only the way numbers are written (a, b) and the fixes in (e). The observation curves that came through a compilation carry that compilation’s own conversions (c). Every change is also written in the warning of each record it touches, and the counts below are made from the records, not typed in (DATA-CHANGES.md).

a · Same numbers, written in the axis units we use

Where a paper plots an equivalent axis, the record converts it exactly. Nothing is fitted or interpolated.

b · One cosmology conversion we made ourselves

{{cvHRecords}} curves ({{cvHSources}}): the released mass functions are densities in units that carry h. They are converted with h = {{cvH}}, the simulation’s own value, so each curve stays in its run’s own cosmology.

c · What the observation compilation did before we received the numbers

{{cvCompN}} observation curves come from the SWIFT/VELOCIraptor comparison-data compilation (calvin-sykes/velociraptor-comparison-data, commit {{cvCompCommit}}), whose scripts re-express each published table in their own conventions. Reading those scripts: {{cvCompH}} are corrected for h to a Planck15 cosmology; {{cvCompImf}} of those also had their stellar masses converted to a Chabrier IMF by the compilation (Kroupa to Chabrier: mass × {{cvKro}}, {{cvKroDex}} dex; Salpeter to Chabrier: × {{cvSal}}, {{cvSalDex}} dex): {{cvImfList}}. {{cvCompNone}} are taken as published and {{cvCompUntraced}} come from a file whose script could not be matched. We keep these values as the compilation made them and record Chabrier IMF and Planck15 as their definitions. We did not re-derive them from the original papers.

d · An optional switch on screen

The Explorer can shift the stellar-mass and SFR axes of simulation curves to a Chabrier IMF (Kroupa {{cvOnKro}} dex, Salpeter {{cvOnSal}} dex). It is off by default, labelled wherever it is on, never applied to a curve whose IMF is not stated, and downloads and the Python loader always hold the published values. These offsets are population averages and differ slightly from the compilation’s factors in (c).

e · Mistakes we fixed, and definitions we added

{{cvCorrN}} fixes to data, analyses or claims are listed, release by release, in the corrections log. {{cvFillRecords}} simulation records carry a definition (for example the stellar-mass aperture) that the table left unstated and the paper states ({{cvFillRules}} sourced rules, §3 and §8); a stated value is never overwritten.

What the analyses and tools do with this

  • The harmonisation study applies IMF offsets and aperture maps inside its own calculation to test whether matching definitions closes the gap between simulated mass functions. It changes no stored curve.
  • The definition-budget analysis measures how large definition differences are. It corrects no curve.
  • The dials page uses the CAMELS simulations as measured (every CAMELS code is measured the same way, so none is converted to match another). The published and observed curves drawn on it are not adjusted to the CAMELS definitions, and the page says so.
  • Check your run does not convert your numbers. It tells you which of your definitions differ from each curve’s.

§5What the Explorer computes

Only exact operations: unit-preserving transforms the reader asks for (per unit mass, times mass, physical axes), residuals against a chosen reference using the plotted points only, and, in the evolution view, reading a published curve at one chosen x inside its plotted range. A simulation with no epoch near the chosen redshift disappears; nothing is interpolated in redshift and nothing is extrapolated.

A simulation can carry dozens of records for one relation (resolutions, model variations, populations, fits). The default view shows one fiducial run per simulation and relation and keeps the rest one click away. The number beside each simulation in the legend is its median offset from a default observational anchor over the x range they share: a picture of where predictions and data differ, not a ranking.

§7Limits

  • Not reviewed by anyone outside. No record has been checked by an outside expert. A single author confirming a suite’s definitions would change the status of its curves.
  • Web fonts. The pages load their fonts from Google, which sends the reader’s address to Google on every visit. Your own curve, in the Explorer’s “Your run”, stays in your browser and is never sent anywhere.
  • {{digitizedShare}} digitized. Digitized curves carry the original authors’ plotting choices, are as precise as the plotted line, and are never rankable; {{rankableShare}} of simulation curves are rankable. Where an author or collaboration releases a table, use that.
  • Not a leaderboard. Many curves cannot be compared like for like, and sim-highline says so instead of scoring them.
  • Thin relations. Relations with three or fewer simulations (count in brackets): {{thinList}}. UV luminosity functions ({{nUvlf}} simulations) and the cosmic SFR density ({{nSfrd}}) are recent additions.
  • Zoom samples (NIHAO, FIRE-2, NewHorizon) are not volume complete.
  • Definition labels are coarse. “Unspecified” hides everything, and two labels that read alike may differ in detail. Observational mocks in simulations are labelled by their definitions; intrinsic and mock quantities are not mixed.
  • Catalogue-level runs for TNG and EAGLE need user credentials and are provided as scripts, not data.

The measured size of the definition problem is in definitions study.
