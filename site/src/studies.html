§2How studies are registered

  • Protocol first. A pre-registered study has a protocol document committed to the repository before its outcome statistics are computed. It fixes the data, the statistic, the decision rule and the sensitivity analyses.
  • What had been looked at. The protocol says what had been examined when it was written (which curves existed, how each suite documents its definitions), so that the reader can judge what the rule could still have been tuned to.
  • Amendments are dated. A change after the protocol is committed is an amendment with its date and a statement of what had been seen when it was made. The protocols of the cosmic star formation study and the all-at-once study were each amended once, before the run, after an independent review.
  • Generated analysis. The analysis document is written by the analysis script from the data, with every departure from the protocol listed as a deviation. The numbers on the study pages are read from the script’s JSON output, not typed.
  • Power. Where a decision rule can be calibrated, its false-positive rate and power are checked on synthetic data before the real data are read (cosmic star formation and all-at-once studies), or reported with the result (mass-function study). The all-at-once calibration uses synthetic worlds with the real block layout whose true distance is known.
  • Null results are reported. A rule that is not met is reported as not met, with the interval, and the page says what the test could and could not have seen.
  • The definitions study has no protocol. It is a descriptive analysis; its limits motivated the mass-function study.

The dated trail

The commit history shows the order. For each pre-registered study: when its protocol was committed, when each amendment was, and when the result file first appeared. The hash is the commit's fingerprint; the amendments say what had been looked at when they were written.
